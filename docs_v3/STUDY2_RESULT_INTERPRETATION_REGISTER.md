# Study 2 result interpretation register

## Permitted headline

Across sixteen analytical source strata, changing the positive-state boundary
from ERP to specialised-or-integrated software and then to any
computer- or platform-enabled method changed both the level and the ordering
of source-stratum estimates.

## Permitted findings

- The broad definition produces substantially higher levels than the two
  narrower definitions.
- The broad positive class is usually dominated by standard software rather
  than ERP.
- A material fraction of source-stratum pair orderings reverses when the
  definition changes.
- Weighting changes some source-stratum estimates materially.
- The broad-versus-narrow ordering survives a conservative unweighted
  unresolved-row audit.

## Mandatory qualifications

- Results are source-stratum-specific descriptive estimates.
- India and India_Wave2_New are separate strata.
- The 986 unresolved rows are excluded from the frozen primary denominator.
- Strict ERP rates are suppressed in two source strata.
- Composition summaries use only disclosure-eligible strata.
- No global prevalence, causal effect, p-value or confidence interval is
  reported.

## Prohibited wording

- global adoption rate;
- fifteen-country prevalence;
- advanced planning prevalence;
- digital transformation rate;
- ERP maturity;
- causal country effects;
- representative international ranking;
- validation of one universally correct outcome definition.

## Preferred terminology

- `integrated planning` only when explicitly defined as ERP being the
  most-used production-planning method;
- `specialised planning` for specialised software or ERP;
- `digitally enabled planning` for any computer- or platform-enabled method;
- `source stratum`, not `country`, when referring to the analytical unit.
