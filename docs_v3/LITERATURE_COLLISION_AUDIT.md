# Literature-collision audit for Outcome-Definition Sensitivity Analysis

## 1. Audit purpose

This audit asks whether Outcome-Definition Sensitivity Analysis (ODSA) collides with an established named method, whether its proposed contribution is already covered by adjacent approaches and which novelty claim can survive a hostile review at *Information & Management*.

The audit is intentionally asymmetric. It does not ask whether ODSA can be described as new. It asks which parts would remain defensible after a reviewer identifies the strongest prior work on alternative operationalisations, measurement nonequivalence, multiverse analysis, specification curves, vibration of effects, misclassification and Information Systems measurement.

## 2. Search scope and limits

Searches were conducted in August 2026 using exact phrases and concept combinations, including:

- `"Outcome-Definition Sensitivity Analysis"`;
- `"outcome-definition sensitivity"`;
- alternative operationalisation or operationalization with multiverse;
- measurement nonequivalence within multiverse analysis;
- categorical outcome sensitivity, outcome misclassification and vibration of effects;
- Information Systems measurement, system-use conceptualisation and categorical indicators.

No established method using the exact name *Outcome-Definition Sensitivity Analysis* was located in the sources examined. This is a bounded search result, not proof that the phrase has never appeared.

## 3. Strongest novelty threats

### 3.1 Multiverse operationalisations

Hanel and Zarzeczna explicitly extend multiverse reasoning to multiple operationalisations of constructs. Their demonstration shows that associations can change substantially across many defensible ways of measuring the same broad concept. This removes any defensible claim that ODSA is the first approach to examine sensitivity to alternative operationalisation.

**Consequence for ODSA:** the contribution cannot be “we run several outcome recodings”. It must concern the special structure of finite categorical state maps and the inference controls attached to them.

### 3.2 Measurement nonequivalence in multiverse analysis

Del Giudice and Gangestad distinguish measurement, effect and power/precision nonequivalence. They warn that alternatives that answer different questions should not be treated as interchangeable members of one multiverse.

**Consequence for ODSA:** ODSA did not discover the general principle that defensible alternatives can be nonequivalent. Its contribution must be framed as a formal, operational implementation for categorical outcome maps.

### 3.3 Current multidisciplinary multiverse guidance

Recent multidisciplinary guidance further distinguishes independent defensibility from equivalence. The existence of such guidance makes it untenable to position ODSA as a general correction to multiverse analysis.

**Consequence for ODSA:** ODSA is complementary. It provides a state-register audit that can determine when outcome-definition branches should be interpreted separately rather than pooled.

### 3.4 Vibration-of-effects frameworks

Vibration-of-effects research already separates instability due to modelling, measurement and sampling choices. It therefore occupies part of the territory that might otherwise be called “sensitivity of conclusions to measurement”.

**Consequence for ODSA:** ODSA must identify a narrower object: the definition-induced transformation of a finite observed state register and its consequences for level, composition, association, subgroup order and admissible claims.

### 3.5 Misclassification sensitivity analysis

Misclassification methods quantify bias when observed categories differ from an assumed underlying truth. ODSA shares category mappings with this literature but addresses a distinct case: each state may be recorded correctly while alternative deliberate definitions still answer different questions.

**Consequence for ODSA:** deliberate definition broadening and observation error must remain separate in notation, software and interpretation.

### 3.6 Information Systems measurement research

Information Systems research already shows that system use, adoption, assimilation and capability depend on careful conceptualisation and operationalisation. IRT and scale-development work in *Information & Management* provides a high methodological benchmark.

**Consequence for ODSA:** it cannot substitute for construct validation, psychometrics or substantive conceptualisation of the system, user, task and organisational context.

## 4. Collision verdict by proposed contribution

| Proposed claim | Verdict | Reason |
|---|---|---|
| ODSA is the first sensitivity analysis for measurement choices | Rejected | Multiverse operationalisations, measurement-nonequivalence and vibration-of-effects work already cover the general problem. |
| ODSA is a new estimator | Rejected | The framework combines registered mappings and established diagnostics rather than estimating a new population parameter. |
| ODSA identifies the correct outcome definition | Rejected | Correctness depends on the construct and question; the method audits consequences and boundaries. |
| ODSA is a universal robustness test | Rejected | It applies to finite categorical state registers and does not resolve construct validity, selection bias or causal identification. |
| ODSA is a formal audit for categorical state-to-outcome maps | Retained | The exact state-register, set-relation, composition, recoverability and claim-admissibility combination was not found as an established integrated method. |
| ODSA formalises claim admissibility for every state counted positive | Retained with caution | This is a conservative necessary condition, not a validity certificate. |
| ODSA connects outcome mapping to subgroup-order and managerial KPI interpretation | Retained with evidence boundary | The contribution is defensible when framed as reporting and inference control, not proven decision improvement. |

## 5. Defensible novelty kernel

The defensible contribution is the integration of six elements:

1. an explicit finite observed-state register;
2. pairwise set relations among outcome definitions;
3. exact accounting of states entering and leaving through symmetric differences;
4. mandatory decomposition of every broader positive class;
5. recoverability checks when the source instrument has coarsened states;
6. a conservative claim-admissibility rule requiring every positive state to be compatible with the wording of the claim.

Association and subgroup-order diagnostics then show how the empirical object changes under those registered maps. The novelty lies in the coupled audit, not in any individual statistic.

## 6. Locked journal-facing claim

> Outcome-Definition Sensitivity Analysis is a formal inference-control audit for finite categorical outcome maps. It links an explicit observed-state register to definition relations, positive-class composition, recoverability, association and subgroup-order diagnostics and a conservative check that every state counted positive remains compatible with the claim attached to the outcome.

The wording “specialised extension” or “formal audit framework” is preferred to “new sensitivity method”.

## 7. Claims prohibited after this audit

The manuscript, cover letter, highlights and repository must not state that ODSA is:

- the first method to study alternative operationalisations;
- the first application of multiverse thinking to measurement;
- a validated scale;
- a causal sensitivity analysis;
- a method for selecting one true adoption rate;
- a replacement for construct validity or measurement invariance;
- evidence that broad measures are inherently invalid;
- a proof that disclosure improves managerial decisions.

## 8. Residual risks

The principal residual novelty risk is that a reviewer may regard the claim-admissibility rule as a restatement of content validity or conceptual alignment. The response must be exact: ODSA does not assess whether states validly measure a latent construct. It audits whether a stated claim is already contradicted by visible states included in the outcome definition.

A second risk is that the empirical application remains simple. Formalisation and simulation can show method behaviour, but independent Study 2 remains necessary to demonstrate portability beyond the Romanian AI item.
