# Information & Management journal-conversation lock

## 1. Purpose

This document fixes the intellectual conversation into which the revised article must enter. It does not substitute for the live *Guide for Authors*, which must be checked again in the final submission interface. It prevents the manuscript from remaining a lightly retargeted technology-management survey article.

## 2. Official journal fit

*Information & Management* presents itself as an international journal of Information Systems theories and applications. Its stated aims include advancing Information Systems methodology and applications and connecting Information Systems development and use to managerial policies, strategies and activities.

The article therefore fits only when ODSA is presented as:

- an Information Systems measurement and inference-control contribution;
- a reusable formal framework rather than a Romanian adoption-rate result;
- a method whose outputs affect the interpretation and governance of IS metrics;
- an empirically demonstrated and independently replicated workflow.

The Romanian AI response set is Study 1. It is not the paper's primary identity.

## 3. Benchmark set from the journal

### 3.1 Methodological benchmark

Rusch et al. do not merely advocate a measurement alternative. They explain the limitations of the prevailing paradigm, formalise the competing approach, demonstrate it empirically, state when it should be used and identify its limitations.

**Required implication:** ODSA must include formal properties, counterexamples, an executable implementation, simulation behaviour, boundary conditions and at least two empirical applications.

### 3.2 Construct-development benchmark

Recent scale-development work in the journal uses a theory-grounded conceptualisation, established development procedures and multi-stage validation.

**Required implication:** ODSA must not claim psychometric validation. Its value must be clearly separated from scale development while matching the journal's expectation of disciplined concept definition and validation evidence.

### 3.3 AI-capability benchmark

The journal's AI-capability research defines the construct, develops its measure and relates it to theoretically meaningful outcomes.

**Required implication:** the article must distinguish engagement states from readiness, capability and realised value. Study 1 cannot be presented as an AI-capability study.

### 3.4 Current conceptual benchmark

Recent conceptual work on agentic AI uses an explicit theoretical lens, separates the focal phenomenon from adjacent constructs and develops a coherent framework with multi-level implications.

**Required implication:** ODSA requires a concise theory of why outcome maps are part of the Information Systems object rather than an after-the-fact coding detail.

### 3.5 Current empirical benchmark

Recent work on human–machine collaboration uses a longitudinal randomised experiment and tests theoretically specified mechanisms.

**Required implication:** the present article should not imitate that design, but it must recognise the evidence gap. Managerial decision improvement cannot be claimed without a dedicated decision experiment.

## 4. Locked article identity

### Working title

**When outcome definitions change the phenomenon: Outcome-Definition Sensitivity Analysis for Information Systems research**

### Article type

Methodological Information Systems article comprising:

1. a formal state–definition–claim framework;
2. mathematical properties and counterexamples;
3. a frozen Monte Carlo simulation programme;
4. Study 1 as an aggregate-only organisational AI application;
5. an independent Study 2;
6. cross-study implications for IS measurement and managerial KPI governance.

### Central question

> How sensitive are the reported level, positive-class composition, association, subgroup order and admissible claims to substantively defensible alternative definitions of a categorical Information Systems outcome?

### Central thesis

Alternative definitions can be individually defensible yet scientifically nonequivalent. A broad definition is not defective merely because it is broad. The error occurs when its label or inference narrows beyond the states counted positive.

## 5. Locked contribution structure

### Contribution 1 — formalisation

ODSA provides an explicit state register, set relations among definitions, exact difference decomposition, composition and recoverability diagnostics and a conservative claim-admissibility rule.

### Contribution 2 — method behaviour

The simulation maps when nested broadening leaves association and subgroup order stable and when it changes them, while separating sampling error from observation-process error.

### Contribution 3 — empirical demonstration and replication

Study 1 provides a bounded worked stress test. Study 2 must establish that the audit is portable to an independent IS context with a substantively meaningful state structure.

### Contribution 4 — managerial reporting

ODSA specifies what KPI designers should disclose: positive states, denominator, positive-class composition, definition-specific associations, subgroup-order implications and the inference boundary. It does not claim that the audit improves decisions unless this is tested separately.

## 6. Literature conversations that must appear in the manuscript

The manuscript must engage five conversations rather than presenting a generic literature review:

1. **IS construct and system-use conceptualisation** — why labels such as adoption, implementation, assimilation and use are not interchangeable;
2. **categorical operationalisation and coarsening** — what distinctions are preserved or destroyed by the instrument;
3. **multiverse and specification traditions** — why defensible definitions may be nonequivalent and should not automatically be pooled;
4. **measurement error and misclassification** — why deliberate broadening differs from erroneous observation;
5. **managerial information quality** — why the same headline percentage can create different governance objects.

## 8. Desk-rejection triggers to remove before submission

The manuscript is not ready while any of the following remains:

- the abstract leads with Romanian AI prevalence rather than the general IS problem;
- ODSA is described as the first analysis of alternative operationalisations;
- Study 2 is missing;
- the simulation is reported without ADEMP structure and Monte Carlo uncertainty;
- the article implies psychometric validation;
- the ethics statement lacks a defensible institutional or legal basis for non-review;
- repository claims exceed aggregate-level reproducibility;
- AI-assisted code and explanatory figures are incompletely disclosed;
- the public release and article figures cannot be reproduced from the same versioned source;
- TASM-specific language, references or strategic-technology framing remains dominant.
